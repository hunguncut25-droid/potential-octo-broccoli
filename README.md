# Kokomo Taxi Service: Business Plan

## Executive summary

Launch a locally operated, phone- and web-bookable taxi service serving Kokomo and nearby Howard County communities. Start with three commercially insured vehicles and scheduled, on-demand local trips. Compete on dependable pickup times, courteous local drivers, clear fare quotes, and a real person customers can call—not on being the cheapest ride in town.

The initial customers are residents without reliable access to a car, shift workers, older adults who can travel independently, visitors, and local businesses that need dependable rides for staff or guests. Private-pay, non-emergency trips are the launch focus. Medical transportation broker work, wheelchair-accessible service, and expansion outside the core area should be added only after their specific operating, insurance, and regulatory requirements are confirmed.

This plan is a starting point, not a market study or legal opinion. Financial figures are illustrative planning assumptions in U.S. dollars, not local quotes or guaranteed results. Validate demand, rates, insurance, vehicle costs, and local requirements before investing.

## Market and opportunity

Kokomo is the county seat and largest population center in Howard County. The U.S. Census Bureau's QuickFacts page is a useful starting point for evaluating the local population and household profile; it does not establish how many people will pay for taxi rides. The service should test actual demand before adding vehicles.

Potential customer groups:

- Residents who do not drive or whose vehicle is temporarily unavailable.
- Employees needing reliable rides to and from work, including early and late shifts.
- Older adults and people who can use a standard vehicle but prefer not to drive.
- Visitors, hotel guests, and customers traveling to restaurants or local events.
- Employers and other organizations that need repeat trips for staff or guests.

Demand and competitive position are hypotheses to verify. Before launch, call or book existing local transportation providers, map their hours and service areas, interview at least 20 prospective customers and 10 business or community partners, and record unmet requests, acceptable wait times, and willingness to pay. Seek recurring business accounts rather than relying only on occasional street or phone hails.

## Services and pricing

Offer:

1. Pre-booked and on-demand local point-to-point rides.
2. Scheduled rides for work shifts, appointments, and community activities.
3. Pre-arranged longer-distance trips, including airport trips, with a written quote.
4. Business accounts with authorized users, trip receipts, and monthly billing after a credit check.

Publish service hours, service boundaries, cancellation terms, and accessible ways to book. Take reservations by phone and a simple mobile-friendly website; add text updates and online booking once the dispatch process is reliable. Quote the total fare before confirming whenever practical.

Do not publish or promise a specific fare schedule until Kokomo's current rules have been checked. Build a proposed meter or zone fare from locally verified requirements and real costs. Track base fare, mileage, waiting time, discounts, and payment fees separately; review fares monthly against fuel, labor, insurance, and vehicle costs.

## Marketing and sales

- Set up a clear website and Google Business Profile with phone number, hours, service area, booking instructions, and review link.
- Distribute cards and service information—with permission—through hotels, employers, apartment managers, senior-serving organizations, and event venues.
- Pitch shift-ride packages to employers and repeat-trip accounts to local businesses.
- Offer a first-ride promotion with a defined end date and measure repeat bookings; avoid discounts that make trips unprofitable.
- Ask satisfied riders for reviews and referrals. Respond promptly to missed pickups or complaints and record corrective actions.
- Track calls, quote-to-booking conversion, completed trips, repeat customers, and acquisition cost by channel each week.

## Operations

Begin with three late-model used sedans selected for reliability, fuel economy, passenger comfort, and affordable parts. Use a commercially insured, inspected fleet with clear vehicle identification and safety equipment. Keep one vehicle available as a spare when possible; do not promise continuous coverage until staffing supports it.

Use a dispatcher/owner to answer calls, schedule trips, monitor pickups, and resolve service issues. Staff trained drivers for defined shifts, verify required driving credentials and background checks, and establish written policies for safe driving, service animals, lost property, incidents, cash handling, customer privacy, and refusing unsafe trips. Provide receipts and multiple payment options.

Maintain a daily vehicle checklist and service log, confirm appointments, communicate delays, and review trip records at the end of every shift. Do not transport passengers who require specialized assistance unless the vehicle, staff training, insurance, and applicable requirements support that service.

## Legal, safety, and insurance checklist

Before taking a fare:

- Form and register the business through Indiana's official INBiz service; confirm tax, employer, and recordkeeping obligations with qualified professionals.
- Contact the City of Kokomo and Howard County to confirm which local permits, operating rules, vehicle inspections, driver qualifications, fare disclosures, and markings apply. Obtain the requirements in writing.
- Confirm vehicle registration and inspection requirements with the relevant Indiana authorities.
- Obtain commercial auto and general liability coverage explicitly covering paid passenger transportation; ask the broker about hired/non-owned vehicles, uninsured motorists, and workers' compensation.
- Confirm employment classification, payroll, background-screening, accessibility, and passenger privacy obligations with qualified advisers.
- Obtain any additional approvals and insurance required before accepting medical, brokered, accessible-vehicle, or interstate trips.

Do not assume a standard personal auto policy, rideshare endorsement, or ordinary business registration is sufficient for a taxi operation.

## Year-one operating model

This base case tests whether a small fleet could cover operating costs; it is not a promise of profit. It assumes three active cars, 12 completed paid trips per car per operating day, 26 operating days per month, and an average collected fare of $24. All four assumptions require a pilot and local validation.

| Monthly base-case calculation | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Trips: 3 cars × 12 trips × 26 days | 936 |
| Gross fares: 936 trips × $24 average collected fare | $22,464 |
| Driver wages and payroll burden | $12,350 |
| Fuel | $1,200 |
| Commercial insurance | $2,400 |
| Vehicle financing/lease reserve | $2,250 |
| Maintenance and tires | $900 |
| Dispatch, phones, and payment tools | $450 |
| Marketing | $500 |
| Administration, permits, and supplies | $500 |
| **Estimated monthly operating costs** | **$20,550** |
| **Estimated monthly operating surplus** | **$1,914** |

The estimated surplus is before income taxes, owner compensation, debt not represented above, and unexpected costs. It is only about 8.5% of gross fares, so a modest decline in trip volume or fare can erase it. Confirm that the wage budget covers actual shifts and applicable labor costs. Recalculate using local insurance and fuel quotes, deadhead mileage, card fees, cancellations, vehicle downtime, taxes, and seasonal demand before deciding whether the model is financeable.

Review three scenarios each month:

- **Downside:** 8 trips per car/day at $22 average fare = $13,728 monthly gross fares; reduce hours and avoid adding vehicles unless costs or contracted demand change.
- **Base:** 12 trips per car/day at $24 average fare = $22,464 monthly gross fares.
- **Upside:** 15 trips per car/day at $26 average fare = $30,420 monthly gross fares; add capacity only after sustained utilization and service quality support it.

At the base assumptions, estimated operating break-even is about **856 paid trips per month**, or approximately **11 trips per vehicle per operating day** (monthly costs of $20,550 ÷ $24 per trip). This simplified threshold excludes owner pay, taxes, and other omitted costs; the real break-even is higher if those costs apply.

## Milestones and measures

**Weeks 1–4 — Validate:** interview customers and business prospects, review competitors, confirm written regulatory requirements, and obtain vehicle/insurance quotes. Proceed only if a conservative forecast covers all costs and working capital.

**Weeks 5–8 — Prepare:** register the business, secure required approvals and insurance, purchase vehicles, hire and train drivers, set up dispatch and payments, and test the full booking-to-receipt process.

**Weeks 9–12 — Pilot:** launch with limited hours and a defined service area. Review every missed pickup, customer complaint, and safety incident; change hours and pricing only after measuring demand and cost.

**Months 4–12 — Improve:** add business accounts and service hours based on repeat bookings. Add a vehicle only after the fleet has sustained high utilization, on-time performance, positive contribution per trip, and adequate cash reserves for at least three months of fixed costs.

Weekly dashboard: completed trips per car/day, average fare, revenue per vehicle-hour, empty/deadhead miles, on-time pickup rate, cancellations, repeat-customer share, cost per trip, complaints/incidents, and cash on hand. Pause expansion if trips fall below break-even or safety and on-time measures deteriorate.

## Key risks and responses

| Risk | Response |
| --- | --- |
| Demand is lower or more seasonal than expected | Pilot limited hours, pursue recurring accounts, and delay fleet expansion. |
| Insurance, labor, or vehicle costs exceed estimates | Get written quotes before launch, maintain a contingency reserve, and reprice or reduce uneconomic hours. |
| Established providers or app-based rides reduce bookings | Differentiate with local phone support, reliable scheduled pickups, and accountable business relationships. |
| Vehicle breakdown or driver absence disrupts service | Preventive maintenance, documented shift coverage, backup arrangements, and honest wait-time estimates. |
| Regulatory or safety noncompliance | Confirm requirements before operation, train staff, maintain records, and review changes with qualified advisers. |
